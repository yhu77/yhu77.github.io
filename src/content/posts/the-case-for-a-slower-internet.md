The internet once felt like a collection of places. You visited a favorite blog, wandered through its links, and returned because a particular voice made the space feel alive.

Now, much of our time online is spent inside feeds designed to erase the feeling of arrival. There is always another post waiting. We consume without choosing and leave without remembering.

## A place, not a stream

A personal website is a small refusal of that logic. It asks the writer to make a place and the reader to visit it deliberately. There is no infinite scroll and no obligation to publish every day. A piece can remain on the front page for a month and still be doing its work.

Slowness is not nostalgia. It is a design choice. It creates enough space for attention to gather around an idea instead of scattering across a hundred fragments.

## Choose what deserves to last

The most useful question may be simple: *Would I still want this to exist a year from now?* That test changes what we publish. It favors reflection over reaction and clarity over urgency.

The slower internet is already here. It lives in newsletters, independent sites, small communities, and the bookmarks we return to. We only have to choose it.
